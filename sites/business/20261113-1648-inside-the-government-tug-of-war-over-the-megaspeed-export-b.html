Inside the Government Tug-of-War Over the Megaspeed Export Ban

13 Nov 2026 · Source: BBC News

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent recently pointed fingers at the Commerce Department, led by Howard Lutnick, accusing the agency of dragging its feet on restricting a company named Megaspeed. During an interview with CNBC, Bessent claimed that multiple other federal divisions had supported adding Megaspeed to a strict government trade restriction registry for an extended period, but the Commerce Department allegedly failed to act in time.

To understand why this friction matters, it helps to look at how trade blacklists function in the United States. The federal government uses an official registry known as the entity list to control which foreign businesses, organizations, or individuals can purchase American technology and goods. Placing a company on this roster typically requires strict licensing requirements or outright bans, acting as a powerful tool to protect national security interests and regulate global commerce.

Because export controls require coordination across multiple branches of the federal government, disagreements over timing and strategy are not uncommon. While the Treasury and other departments may view a company as an immediate risk, the Commerce Department often has to weigh complex economic consequences, diplomatic ties, and the broader impact on domestic industries before implementing formal trade restrictions. This bureaucratic balancing act frequently leads to visible friction among high-ranking administration officials.

Observers and market participants will likely monitor future trade policy announcements to see how the administration resolves these internal debates regarding export restrictions.
